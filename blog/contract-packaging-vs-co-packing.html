Contract Packaging vs. Co-Packing — What's the Difference?

Ole Hovde·2026-09-17

If you've been researching production options for your supplement or food brand, you've encountered both terms: co-packing and contract packaging. Industry people use them interchangeably. They shouldn't. The distinction matters for what service you actually need.

The Definitions

Co-Packing (Contract Manufacturing)

A co-packer (in the full sense of the term) receives your raw materials — bulk ingredients — and produces your finished product. They may blend, fill, seal, label, and kit your product from raw inputs to finished goods.

When most supplement brands say they're looking for a co-packer, they mean this: a facility that takes their formula and ingredients and turns them into finished, market-ready pouches.

Contract Packaging

A contract packager receives your already-manufactured or already-blended product and packages it into its final form. They're not making anything — they're putting a finished or semi-finished product into retail-ready packaging.

Example: you have a pre-blended protein powder. You ship it to a contract packager. They fill it into pouches, apply labels, and ship it to your warehouse. They didn't formulate or blend anything — they just packaged what you sent.

Which One Do You Need?

  • You need a co-packer if: You have a formula and raw ingredients, and you need someone to blend and fill them into finished pouches.
  • You need a contract packager if: You already have a finished or pre-blended product and just need someone to put it into retail packaging.
  • You need both if: Your formula requires manufacturing beyond blending — tablet pressing, encapsulation, liquid filling, etc. — plus packaging into pouches or bottles.

Why the Terminology Gets Confusing

The industry uses "co-packer" to mean both things, depending on who's talking. A large CPG brand that does its own manufacturing might hire a "co-packer" to handle packaging only. A supplement startup that has a formula but no production capability means something entirely different by the same word.

When you're evaluating facilities, ask explicitly: "Do you do blending and filling, or just filling?" That question cuts through the terminology confusion and tells you exactly what you're getting.
