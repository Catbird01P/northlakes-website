How Much Does a Co-Packer Cost? A Real Fee Breakdown

Ole Hovde·2026-09-12·7 min read

Search for co-packer pricing and you'll mostly find one answer: "call for a quote." That's not because pricing is impossible to explain — it's because most shops treat it as leverage. Here's what actually goes into a co-packing quote, using our own published rate card as a real, worked example.

The cost components behind every quote

Whoever you talk to, a co-packing quote is built from some combination of these pieces:

  • Machine setup / changeover: the labor and downtime to configure a line for your specific pouch size, fill weight, and formula.
  • Blending labor: combining your formula to spec, often priced per pound and scaled by how many ingredients and how fine a mix you need.
  • Fill & pack labor: running the actual production, typically rolled into a per-unit or flat run rate.
  • Packaging materials: film, pouches, or stick pack stock — either supplied by you or sourced through the copacker (usually at a markup if they source it).
  • Minimum job charge: a floor price that exists because a changeover costs roughly the same whether you're running 500 units or 5,000.

Fees that don't show up until the invoice

The fees above are the ones a copacker will happily tell you about. The ones worth asking about directly, because they're where surprise invoices come from:

  • Rescheduling fees for moving a booked production date.
  • Cancellation fees if you cancel close to your scheduled run — once a slot is booked, the line time is a real cost to the copacker whether or not your product runs through it.
  • Storage fees for finished goods or raw materials sitting in their warehouse.
  • Markups on sourced packaging or ingredients, if you're not supplying your own.

Ask for these numbers in writing before you sign, not after your first invoice.

How to compare quotes apples-to-apples

Two copackers quoting the same run can look wildly different until you normalize for what's actually included. Before comparing numbers, confirm each quote covers: machine setup, blending labor, and the full production run — not just fill-and-pack. Then check separately for MOQ, changeover fees, and cancellation/reschedule terms. A lower headline number with a higher MOQ and a punitive cancellation fee isn't actually cheaper.
