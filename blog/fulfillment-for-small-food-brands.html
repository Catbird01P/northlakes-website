Fulfillment for Small Food and Supplement Brands — What to Look For

Ole Hovde·2026-10-24

The fulfillment industry is built around volume. The major 3PLs — the ones with the slick websites, the automated robots, the real-time dashboards — are optimized for brands doing thousands of orders a month. Their economics work at that scale. Their pricing structures, their minimum commitments, their technology overhead — all of it is sized for a brand that has already figured out product-market fit and is scaling hard.

If you’re doing 10 to 50 orders a day, you are not their customer. You’re an afterthought in their model, and you’ll be priced like one.

What Small Brand Fulfillment Actually Needs to Be

Simple. That’s the answer. You don’t need a robot. You don’t need a real-time inventory dashboard with 47 metrics. You need someone to pick your orders correctly, get them out the same day, charge you a fair rate, and pick up the phone when something goes wrong.

For a food or supplement brand, you also need the storage environment to be appropriate for your product. Temperature and humidity controlled. Off-floor racking. Pest control. Lot traceability so you can trace any unit that shipped to any customer if you ever need to. That’s the full list. Everything else is a feature built to justify a higher price point to brands that don’t need it.

The Questions to Ask Before You Sign Anything

Is there a monthly minimum or platform fee? If your volume is 30 orders a month and their minimum billing is $500 a month, you’re paying for orders you’re not shipping.

Is shipping passed through at actual carrier cost? This is the question most brands never ask because they assume the 3PL is passing rates through. Many aren’t. Ask directly and get the answer in writing.

What are your inbound receiving fees? Some 3PLs charge $50–200 per pallet just to accept your delivery. That’s real money on every shipment.

Are there per-unit charges for bulk case movement? This is the one that generated a $12,000 invoice in my own experience. Find out before you have a large outbound order that triggers it.

What is your cancellation term? A 90-day cancellation notice on a month-to-month contract is very different from a 12-month minimum commitment.

What Good Fulfillment Looks Like at Small Scale

We handle fulfillment for brands doing anywhere from single-digit orders per week to several hundred per month. $45 per pallet per month for storage. $3 per order for pick and pack. Carrier cost on shipping — no markup if you pay weekly.

No platform fee. No receiving fee. No monthly minimum. No per-unit bulk movement charges. No mystery fees that show up on an invoice and require a 20-minute call to understand. You know your costs before your first pallet arrives. That’s how it should work. — Ole
