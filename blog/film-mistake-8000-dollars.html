The Film Mistake That Costs Brands $8,000 Before a Single Unit Runs

Ole Hovde·2026-10-21

I’ve had this conversation more times than I want to count. A brand calls us excited. Their formula is finalized. Their label artwork is done. They went ahead and ordered their film — got a great deal on 100,000 impressions from a printer they found online. They’re ready to run.

Then I ask three questions. What’s the core width? What’s the maximum roll diameter? And which direction does the film unwind?

Sometimes they know. More often, they go quiet.

Why These Questions Matter

A form-fill-seal machine is a mechanical system with very specific requirements. The film has to feed through it in a precise way for the machine to form pouches, fill them, seal them, and cut them correctly. If the film isn’t spec’d to the machine, it doesn’t work. It’s that simple.

Core width is the diameter of the cardboard tube the film is wound around. Most machines run either a 3-inch or 6-inch core. Order the wrong one and the roll won’t even mount on the machine.

Maximum roll diameter is how large the roll itself can be. There’s a physical limit to what the machine can accept. A roll that’s too large won’t fit. A roll that’s too small means you’re constantly stopping to swap rolls mid-run.

Unwind direction is the one that gets people most often. It refers to which way the film comes off the roll as it feeds into the machine. The eye marks — the small registration marks the machine’s sensors use to align each pouch — need to be on a specific side. Get it backwards and the machine can’t read the marks. Every pouch is misaligned. The run is unusable.

What This Actually Costs You

Custom-printed roll stock film is not cheap. At typical print quantities you’re looking at real money per thousand impressions. And it’s not returnable. You spec’d it, you printed it, it’s yours.

If you ordered the wrong unwind direction, you have a pallet of unusable packaging material. If you ordered the wrong core width, same story. The film is perfectly printed and completely worthless for your co-packer’s machine.

I’ve seen brands eat $6,000, $8,000, even more on film that had to be scrapped because they ordered before they confirmed specs with their co-packer. Every single one of them told me the same thing: they didn’t know to ask.

The Part That Makes It Worse

Film has a 10–12 week lead time from approved artwork to delivery if you’re going through a reputable digital printer. You’ve now lost weeks of lead time on top of the money. You push your launch. You potentially lose your production slot. You spend more money getting replacement film on a rush. All for a mistake that takes five minutes to prevent.

The five-minute fix

Before you talk to a single film printer, call your co-packer and get four things in writing: core width, maximum roll diameter, unwind direction, and eye mark placement. Then send those specs to your film supplier before a single pixel goes to print. That’s the whole prevention plan.
