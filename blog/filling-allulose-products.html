INGREDIENT GUIDE

Filling Allulose-Based Products — What Co-Packers Won't Tell You

Ole Hovde·2026-09-17

We get more questions about allulose than almost any other ingredient. Brands have heard horror stories — clumping, flavor pickup, machine jams — and want to know what's real before they commit to a formula. Here's what's actually true.

What Makes Allulose Challenging

Allulose is a rare sugar that the body processes differently from sucrose. It tastes like sugar, has about 70% of the sweetness, and contributes negligible calories because it's not metabolized normally. That makes it increasingly popular in supplement and functional food products.

Its challenge in powder manufacturing comes from one property: it is significantly more hygroscopic than most other sweeteners. Allulose aggressively absorbs moisture from the surrounding environment. In an uncontrolled setting — open containers, humid storage, slow production environments — this causes:

  • Clumping and caking that affects flow through auger fill systems
  • Bridging in hoppers that disrupts consistent fill weights
  • Seal contamination if the powder absorbs moisture and becomes sticky before the seal cycle

What Doesn't Actually Happen in a Controlled Production Environment

Here's where the allulose reputation gets exaggerated. We've heard brands worried that allulose will "pick up flavors from the room" or absorb ambient odors during production. Let's address this directly.

In a production environment like North Lakes', the powder is in open air for seconds — not hours. The sequence is: blend arrives sealed → hopper loaded → auger doses into pouch → heat seal closes the pouch. The exposure window is measured in fractions of a second per fill cycle.

The "picks up room flavor" concern is valid in storage and transit scenarios — open bags of allulose sitting near strong-smelling products for extended periods. It is not a meaningful concern in a properly run production environment where the product goes from sealed container to sealed pouch in seconds.

Allulose picks up ambient flavor from prolonged exposure. In a production run, exposure is measured in seconds — not hours.

What Happens When Allulose Is in a Blend

The hygroscopic behavior of allulose changes significantly when it's blended with other ingredients. In a typical whey protein + allulose formula at 15–20% allulose inclusion:

  • The whey protein matrix surrounds and coats allulose particles
  • The protein coating reduces direct air exposure at the particle level
  • The blend as a whole has different moisture-scavenging behavior than pure allulose

We ran a real-world example: a 53g fill with 10g allulose in a whey protein base. At 19% allulose by weight, this formula ran without meaningful clumping or flow issues. Our facility regularly runs freeze-dried powders — some of the most hygroscopic ingredients in the category — without flow problems. A standard whey/allulose blend at normal inclusion rates is not a red flag for our machine and setup.

What Actually Matters When Running Allulose

The real precautions for allulose-containing formulas are about storage and handling, not production:

Ingredient storage before the run

Your allulose should arrive in moisture-barrier packaging — sealed bags or containers with desiccant. If it's been stored in a humid environment or exposed to air, it may have already started absorbing moisture. A clumped, partially caked allulose ingredient is going to cause more problems on the machine than fresh, properly stored material.

Blend storage between blending and filling

At North Lakes, we blend immediately before filling — we don't let blended product sit open for extended periods. For allulose-containing blends, this is especially important. The blended product should go into the hopper promptly after blending.

COA moisture content

Check your allulose COA for moisture content. If the ingredient has picked up moisture in transit or storage, the moisture content reading on your COA will show it. Elevated moisture in an allulose ingredient is a signal to discuss with your co-packer before the run.

Know your other hygroscopic ingredients

If your formula combines allulose with other hygroscopic ingredients — certain fiber blends, some flavor systems, freeze-dried components — the cumulative hygroscopic behavior of the blend can be more significant than allulose alone. Tell your co-packer the full ingredient list, not just the allulose inclusion rate.

The Bottom Line

Allulose is runnable. It requires reasonable precautions — proper storage, prompt blending-to-filling, good COA documentation — that are standard practice for any powder with hygroscopic components. The horror stories about allulose are mostly stories about poor storage, poor handling, or production environments that weren't built for this kind of ingredient.

If you have a whey protein + allulose formula and you're concerned about running it, the answer is a small blend test before your full production run. We'll see exactly how your specific formula flows, and we'll know what to expect before we commit your full ingredient quantity to the machine.
