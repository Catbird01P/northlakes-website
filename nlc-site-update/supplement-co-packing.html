What does 21 CFR 111 require from a supplement co-packer?

Part 111 is FDA's current good manufacturing practice rule for dietary supplements. For a powder co-packer, the parts that matter most are:

RequirementHow we meet it
Master manufacturing record (§111.205)One MMR per product: formula, specs, fill target and limits, tests. You approve it before the first run.
Identity testing (§111.75(a)(1))Every lot of every dietary ingredient we blend. A supplier COA alone doesn't count.
COA verification (§111.75(a)(2))First lot from each supplier, then yearly.
Finished batch testing (§111.75(c))Every batch, per the tests in your MMR.
Batch recordsA batch production record for every lot, reviewed by QC.
Quality control releaseQC releases components and finished batches. Nothing ships unreleased.
Reserve samples (§111.83)At least twice the quantity needed for testing, kept 1 year past shelf life or 2 years from distribution.
RecordsKept 1 year past shelf life or 2 years from distribution.

The regulation itself is at 21 CFR Part 111 on eCFR.

What does supplement setup cost, and why?

$500 for a new supplement formula, and $150 for each added flavor. FDA’s supplement rule (21 CFR 111) requires us to build a master manufacturing record, review your specifications and approve your suppliers before we make anything. Each flavor is its own formula with its own record, but most of the work carries over, so added flavors cost less. Identity testing is billed separately at the lab’s price.

Who is responsible for what?

You own the formula, label claims, overages, shelf life and stability, and adverse event reporting. We own making the product to your MMR and releasing it against your specs. When a customer complaint comes to us, QC reviews it and tells you within 1 business day.

What does identity testing cost?

The lab’s price plus shipping, with no markup: at the labs we use, about $80–$700 per dietary ingredient lot, depending on the method each ingredient needs. We confirm it on your quote. If you send us a finished blend that we only fill and pack, identity testing of ingredients doesn't apply; we need your blend spec. More in our identity testing guide.

What supplement powders do you run?

Protein, collagen, creatine, electrolytes, greens, pre-workout, sleep and focus blends, and other dry powder supplements in sachets, pouches and, from December 2026, stick packs. Low-dose actives are premixed before blending, and blend uniformity is tested on the first batch and after any change. See powder blending.

Are you GMP certified?

Part 111 doesn't require third-party GMP certification, and we don't claim one. We run to Part 111 and the SQF Fundamentals code, keep the records to prove it, and invite you to audit us. Our quality page covers what we'll show you.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need identity testing if I send a finished blend?

No. If we only fill and pack your pre-blended product, we do a visual and document check against your blend spec instead.

Who files adverse event reports?

The brand owner. We forward any complaint to you within 1 business day after QC review.

Can I audit your facility?

Yes. You can review our full SOPs on site. We send a document index and our FDA registration confirmation on request.

How long does a first supplement run take?

Standard turnaround is 30 days from a signed quote. First supplement runs need time for the MMR, specs and ingredient testing, so plan 4–5 weeks.
